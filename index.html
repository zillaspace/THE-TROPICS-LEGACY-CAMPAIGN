The Tropics
Legacy Campaign

A capital-raising and community-engagement platform for The Tropics Hotel / Caliente Tropics in Palm Springs — built around tiki heritage, named places, donor pride, and a permanent house ritual.

The Big Idea

Do not sell names on random hotel parts. Sell permanent participation in a Palm Springs legend.

The Tropics opened in the mid-century tiki era and still has a rare, emotionally resonant story: Palm Springs glamour, Polynesian fantasy, desert modernism, the pool culture, the Reef Bar, and the original tiki carvings. The campaign should feel less like a donor wall and more like an invitation to be woven into the property’s living mythology.

Campaign theme: “Adopt a Piece of Palm Springs History.”
KeepOriginal tiki stewardship, named districts, wellness naming, a Legends Walk, and a founding cocktail.
DropGeneric bronze/silver/gold donor language, random bathroom naming, and anything that cheapens the heritage.
ProtectThe original carvings remain historic assets; donors sponsor stewardship, not ownership.

The Historic Tiki Collection

The strongest known narrative: Ed Crissman, associated with Oceanic Arts, carved original tikis for the Palm Springs Tropics / Caliente Tropics in the 1960s. Sources vary between roughly 20 and 24 original tikis; for campaign purposes, verify the exact current onsite count with a physical inventory before publishing a number.

Recommended structure: plaque sponsorship on the original tikis, not renaming or selling the tikis themselves.

Wording matters: use “Historic Tiki #7 — Stewarded by the Johnston Family,” not “The Johnston Tiki.” The historic identity stays intact, while the donor receives visible legacy.

Ballpark revenue from 24 tikis: $750,000 to $1.5 million depending on tiering and scarcity. A premium allocation could be 4 at $100K, 10 at $50K, and 10 at $25K, totaling $1.15 million.

New Legacy Tiki Collection

Because Oceanic Arts has closed, new carvings should be treated as a curated commission program, not commodity décor. The best approach is to use a mix of respected U.S. tiki artists for provenance and selective international carving partners where appropriate.

U.S. Master CarversHigher cost, better provenance, strongest with tiki collectors and Modernism Week audiences.
FijiBest if the story is authentic and the carvers are credited. Use for ceremonial or ancestral-pillar concepts.
IndonesiaPotentially economical for volume, but avoid anything that feels generic, mass-produced, or disconnected from the property story.

Recommendation: commission fewer, better pieces. A 12-piece “Legacy Tiki Collection” with named donors is stronger than 50 generic imports.

The Permanent House Cocktail

Name: The 1964 Tropics Mai Tai

Positioning: The drink that never leaves the menu. Served at every opening, anniversary, donor dinner, and Legends Walk ceremony.

Recipe

  • 1 oz aged Jamaican rum
  • 1 oz agricole-style rum or aged Martinique-style rum
  • 3/4 oz fresh lime juice
  • 1/2 oz orange curaçao
  • 1/2 oz orgeat
  • 1/4 oz demerara syrup
  • Mint crown, lime shell, and branded swizzle

Ritual: Every donor at $100K+ receives a numbered “1964 Tropics Mai Tai” mug at the grand opening. The first toast is made at sunset by the pool.

Menu line: “Our permanent house cocktail — a desert-island toast to the original 1964 Tropics.”
