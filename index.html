A capital strategy disguised as legacy, art and belonging

This idea does two powerful things at once. It raises capital without diluting ownership, and it creates emotional ownership among investors, donors, local families and resort guests. The Tropics is not just selling names on rooms. It is offering people a permanent place in the story of one of Palm Springs most recognizable tiki landmarks.

The visual signature

The strongest campaign image is not a wall of donor names. It is a resort landscape filled with tiki guardians, torchlight, bronze plaques and stories that guests photograph for decades.

The positioning

Not a hotel. A living artifact.

The property should be presented as one of Palm Springs remaining authentic Polynesian and tiki landmarks. It has mid century atmosphere, resort nostalgia, tiki culture and the rare quality of already feeling like a story.

When presenting this to investors, frame it less as a capital campaign and more as Adopt a Piece of Palm Springs History. For a destination hospitality project, Heritage Circle feels more permanent and prestigious than generic giving categories.

The big idea

Founders of the Village

Commission 50 carved ancestral pillars, also presented as legacy tikis, garden guardians and resort markers.

At $25,000 to $50,000 per pillar, 50 pillars could generate $1,250,000 to $2,500,000 while becoming a visual signature of the property.

Wellness strategy

This can attract donors outside traditional hotel investors.

Where to make 50 authentic tikis

Use three sourcing lanes: United States master provenance for the most important pieces, Fiji for story and cultural resonance, and Bali or Java for scalable resort volume.

Option one

United States master carvers

Best for provenance

Bosko Hrnjak is associated with the modern rebirth of tiki as sculpture and is centered in Southern California.

Crazy Al Evans is a Southern California tiki artist and founder of TIKIMANIA.

Mike Gecko Souirielle is a Hawaii based artist producing tiki mugs and carvings through Gecko’z South Sea Arts.

John Mulder of Eekum Bookum is better suited for donor mugs, plaques and commemorative objects than monumental outdoor carvings.

Option two

Fiji master carvers

Best for story

Create The Tropics Ancestors Project. Commission Fijian carvers through resort cultural centers, village networks or a dedicated field producer.

Each donor receives the story of the carving, certificate of origin, photo documentation and dedication plaque.

This feels more authentic than ordinary container decor and creates a powerful grand opening story.

Custom budget required

Option three

Bali and Java production

Best for scale

Bali and Java can produce large quantities of hand carved resort art. This is the practical source for volume pieces, garden markers, palapa details and lower tier sponsored tikis.

Suggested use: produce the 50 piece village collection with two or three hero pieces by United States or Fijian master carvers, then use Bali or Java for supporting pieces.

Often under $5,000 landed for many large pieces
